\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ch:literature_review}

\section{Overview}
Developing an autonomous mobile fitness tracking platform requires mathematical and empirical grounding across multiple interconnected disciplines: satellite geodesy, optimal stochastic state estimation, cross-platform environmental context detection, inertial gait biomechanics, spatial--temporal trajectory mining, and exercise physiology. This chapter reviews the peer-reviewed literature underpinning Aetron's algorithmic architecture, contrasting theoretical models against commodity mobile operating system constraints and formalizing the research gaps that guide our implementation.

\section{Outdoor GNSS Tracking and Geodetic Kinematics}
\label{sec:gnss_kinematics}
Outdoor fitness tracking relies on Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) to estimate geographic coordinates over time. In a local coordinate frame, positions are expressed as geodetic latitude $\phi$, longitude $\lambda$, and ellipsoidal altitude $h$ referenced to the World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS84) reference ellipsoid.

\subsection{Great-Circle and Ellipsoidal Distance Formulations}
Calculating cumulative distance along a discrete trajectory $\mathcal{T} = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_N\}$ requires integrating incremental distances between consecutive coordinate pairs $p_k = (\phi_k, \lambda_k)$ and $p_{k+1} = (\phi_{k+1}, \lambda_{k+1})$. On a spherical earth model of mean radius $R_{\text{earth}} \approx 6{,}371{,}000$\,m, the great-circle distance is computed using the Haversine formula~\cite{sinnott1984virtues}:
\begin{align}
    \Delta \phi &= \phi_{k+1} - \phi_k, \quad \Delta \lambda = \lambda_{k+1} - \lambda_k \\
    a &= \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \phi}{2}\right) + \cos(\phi_k)\cos(\phi_{k+1})\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \lambda}{2}\right) \\
    d_{\text{haversine}}(p_k, p_{k+1}) &= 2 R_{\text{earth}} \cdot \arctan2\left(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{1 - a}\right)
\end{align}
While computationally efficient, the spherical assumption introduces a systematic geodetic distortion of up to $0.55\%$ depending on latitude. For surveyor-grade ground-truth validation, geodesics are modeled on the WGS84 oblate spheroid ($a = 6{,}378{,}137.0$\,m, $f = 1/298.257223563$) via Vincenty's inverse algorithm~\cite{vincenty1975direct}. In athletic GPS validation studies, Scott et al.~\cite{scott2016validity} categorize cumulative distance Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) as \textbf{Good} ($<5\%$), \textbf{Moderate} ($5$--$10\%$), or \textbf{Poor} ($>10\%$), while Gilgen-Ammann et al.~\cite{gilgenammann2020wrist} benchmark commercial sport watches at $0.9$--$4.1\%$ error on open tracks and $3.9$--$8.9\%$ in urban environments.

\subsection{Sources of GNSS Degradation and Dilution of Precision}
Consumer smartphone GNSS receivers suffer from ionospheric/tropospheric delay, urban canyon non-line-of-sight (NLOS) multipath reflections ($10$--$50$\,m outliers), and poor satellite constellation geometry quantified by Horizontal Dilution of Precision (HDOP):
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\text{pos}} = \text{HDOP} \cdot \sigma_{\text{pr}}
\end{equation}
When HDOP exceeds $3.0$ in narrow street corridors, horizontal variance escalates rapidly and raw coordinate integration inflates total workout distance.

\section{GNSS Filtering, Trajectory Preprocessing, and Optimal Estimation}
\label{sec:filtering_theory}
Raw GNSS streams cannot be directly integrated into athletic telemetry without producing jagged polylines and inflated distances. Modern trajectory pipelines therefore combine kinematic preprocessing with stochastic state estimation~\cite{zheng2015trajectory,li2025trajectory}.

\subsection{Kinematic Preprocessing and Stay-Point Mining}
Zheng~\cite{zheng2015trajectory} and Li et al.~\cite{li2025trajectory} structure GNSS preprocessing into sequential outlier rejection, median spike suppression, stay-point detection, and outage segmentation. A first-stage kinematic gate discards fixes violating modality-specific physical constraints:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Gate}(p_k, p_{k-1}, \Delta t) =
    \begin{cases}
        \text{Reject} & \text{if } \sigma_{\text{accuracy}} > \tau_{\text{acc}} \quad (\text{e.g., } 25\,\text{m}) \\
        \text{Reject} & \text{if } \frac{d(p_k, p_{k-1})}{\Delta t} > v_{\text{max}} \quad (\text{modality-specific}) \\
        \text{Accept} & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For stationary dwell identification, Zheng et al.~\cite{zheng2009mining} define stay points as temporally contiguous sub-trajectories remaining within distance threshold $D_{\max}$ for at least $T_{\min}$, while Ester et al.~\cite{ester1996dbscan} introduce density-based spatial clustering (DBSCAN), which adapts naturally to time-ordered GPS dwell suppression (T-DBSCAN) and can be evaluated on real-world stop/trip diaries such as STAGA~\cite{spang2022staga}.

\subsection{Standard Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) Formulation}
The Kalman Filter~\cite{kalman1960filter} provides the minimum mean-square error (MMSE) estimator for linear Gaussian systems; the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) extends this framework to nonlinear planar kinematics via first-order Taylor linearization~\cite{gelb1974applied,groves2013principles}. Aetron employs a 4-state kinematic vector in a local Equirectangular tangent plane:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} p_x & p_y & v & \theta \end{bmatrix}^T \in \mathbb{R}^4
\end{equation}
where $p_x$ and $p_y$ denote local East and North meters, $v$ is ground speed (m/s), and $\theta$ is track heading (radians clockwise from North). Over step $\Delta t$, state prediction and Jacobian propagation follow:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{x}_k^- &= \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{k-1}) = \begin{bmatrix}
        p_{x, k-1} + v_{k-1} \sin(\theta_{k-1}) \Delta t \\
        p_{y, k-1} + v_{k-1} \cos(\theta_{k-1}) \Delta t \\
        v_{k-1} \\
        \theta_{k-1}
    \end{bmatrix} \\
    \mathbf{F}_k &= \left.\frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\right|_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}} = \begin{bmatrix}
        1 & 0 & \sin(\theta_{k-1})\Delta t & v_{k-1}\cos(\theta_{k-1})\Delta t \\
        0 & 1 & \cos(\theta_{k-1})\Delta t & -v_{k-1}\sin(\theta_{k-1})\Delta t \\
        0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0 & 1
    \end{bmatrix} \\
    \mathbf{P}_k^- &= \mathbf{F}_k \mathbf{P}_{k-1} \mathbf{F}_k^T + \mathbf{Q}_k
\end{align}
With observation $\mathbf{z}_k = [z_{px}, z_{py}, z_v]^T$ (or 4-element with Doppler course when above speed deadband), innovation $\boldsymbol{\nu}_k = \mathbf{z}_k - \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{x}_k^-$, innovation covariance $\mathbf{S}_k = \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T + \mathbf{R}_k$, and gain $\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T \mathbf{S}_k^{-1}$, Aetron updates state covariance strictly via the numerically stable \textbf{Joseph form}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}_k = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k) \mathbf{P}_k^- (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k)^T + \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{R}_k \mathbf{K}_k^T
    \label{eq:joseph_form}
\end{equation}
guaranteeing symmetry and positive semi-definiteness across mobile floating-point pipelines. During stationary pauses ($v < 0.3$\,m/s), a Zero-Velocity Update (ZUPT) applies pseudo-measurement $z_v = 0$ and damps $\mathbf{Q}_k$ to arrest multipath creep.

\subsection{Adaptive EKF (AEKF), Mahalanobis Gating, and Turn-Rate Scaling}
To suppress multipath spikes without rejecting genuine athletic maneuvers, Groves~\cite{groves2013principles} and maneuvering-target tracking surveys~\cite{rongli2003survey} couple innovation-consistency testing with adaptive covariance scaling. Under Gaussian assumptions, the 2-DOF spatial Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS) follows a $\chi^2_2$ distribution:
\begin{equation}
    \text{NIS}_k = \boldsymbol{\nu}_k^T \mathbf{S}_k^{-1} \boldsymbol{\nu}_k
    \label{eq:nis_lit}
\end{equation}
Against $\chi^2_{2, 0.95} = 5.991$ and $\chi^2_{2, 0.999} = 13.816$, moderate outliers inflate $\mathbf{R}_k^{\text{adaptive}} = \max(1.0, \text{NIS}_k / 5.991)\,\mathbf{R}_k$ while severe jumps are gated unless consecutive innovations confirm re-anchoring. Complementarily, centripetal turn-rate scaling~\cite{rongli2003survey} inflates heading process noise when circular turn rate $\dot{\psi}_k$ exceeds modality thresholds, preventing corner overshoot. Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} rigorously evaluates both isolated NIS scaling and full Phase~1+2 integration on pedestrian RTK~\cite{tuwien2026gnss} and cycling snapshot GNSS~\cite{beuchert2023snappergps}.

\section{Indoor/Outdoor Detection (IOD) and Cross-Platform Transition Modeling}
\label{sec:iod_literature}
Reliable dual-mode fitness tracking requires distinguishing outdoor GNSS-trackable segments from indoor dead-reckoning segments across both Android and iOS hardware~\cite{feriol2020review}.

\subsection{Foundational Multi-Sensor IOD: IODetector, Semi-Supervised, and GNSS Schemes}
Early smartphone IOD frameworks established lightweight multi-sensor fusion without heavy deep networks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{IODetector (Li et al., 2014):} Li et al.~\cite{li2014iodetector} formulated a modular service fusing light intensity, cellular RSSI variability, and magnetic field variance via confidence-weighted posterior aggregation, demonstrating that combining orthogonal weak cues substantially outperforms single-sensor thresholding.
    \item \textbf{Semi-Supervised Smartphone IOD (Radu et al., 2014):} Radu et al.~\cite{radu2014semisupervised} highlighted cross-device and cross-building domain shifts when deploying supervised classifiers trained on a single handset, motivating feature normalization and multi-session disjoint evaluation.
    \item \textbf{GNSS-Based IOD (Bui et al., 2020):} Bui et al.~\cite{bui2020gpsiod} demonstrated that GNSS signal quality indicators (satellite visibility, SNR, and reported horizontal positioning accuracy / fix availability) provide high-value indoor/outdoor discrimination, even when only OS-level location accuracy and fix silence are exposed.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Sensor Environmental Context Survey (F{\'e}riol et al., 2020):} F{\'e}riol et al.~\cite{feriol2020review} systematically categorized environmental context cues---including GNSS availability, magnetometers, barometers (pressure deltas across building doors/HVAC envelopes), and inertial dynamics---emphasizing OS privilege constraints in consumer deployment.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deep Learning and Transition Frameworks: DeepIOD, CIOD, and Yoo (2025)}
Dastagir et al.~\cite{dastagir2024deepiod} introduced \textbf{DeepIOD}, combining three 10-second sliding-window neural architectures---Model~A (1D-CNN+LSTM), Model~B (1D-CNN+Multi-Head Attention), and Model~C (2D Depthwise-Separable CNN)---over 8 smartphone channels (\texttt{RSRP}, \texttt{RSRQ}, \texttt{Light}, \texttt{Mag}, \texttt{Acc}, \texttt{Sound}, \texttt{Proximity}, \texttt{Daytime}) via Approximate Majority Voting (AMV):
\begin{align}
    P_{\text{AMV}} &= w_A P_A(\mathbf{x}) + w_B P_B(\mathbf{x}) + w_C P_C(\mathbf{x}), \quad (w_A, w_B, w_C) = (0.345, 0.321, 0.334)
\end{align}
Bakirtzis et al.~\cite{bakirtzis2022ciod} released the large-scale \textbf{Cambridge CIOD} multivariate time-series benchmark ($>1.47$\,M 1\,Hz epochs across 85 sessions), enabling session-disjoint generalization audits. Focusing on doorway crossings, Yoo~\cite{yoo2025seamless} formulated a probabilistic transition detector driven by GNSS carrier-to-noise density ($30 \le C/N_0 \le 40$\,dB-Hz) and temporal smoothing to prevent boundary flip-flopping.

\subsection{Light-Free iOS-Compatible IOD: MagIO, Activity Dynamics, Straightness, and Causal HMM}
A critical gap in prior deep IOD deployments is \textbf{Apple iOS sandboxing}: third-party iOS apps cannot read the ambient light photodiode, raw GNSS $C/N_0$, cellular RSRP/RSRQ, or Wi-Fi RSSI scans. Four peer-reviewed works directly enable a light-free, unprivileged iOS/Android sensing pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MagIO (Ashraf et al., 2018):} Ashraf, Hur, and Park~\cite{ashraf2018magio} proved that ferromagnetic steel and rebar inside buildings induce strong spatial/temporal distortions in smartphone magnetometer readings ($\|\mathbf{m}\| = \sqrt{m_x^2 + m_y^2 + m_z^2}$), whereas outdoor geomagnetic fields remain smoother and closer to the Earth's local dipole ($\approx 25$--$65\,\mu\text{T}$). Extracting distributional statistics (mean, standard deviation, min/max, range, median, skewness, kurtosis, interquartile range, and magnetic energy/derivative) over sliding windows separates indoor and outdoor states using only the magnetometer.
    \item \textbf{Physical Activity Dynamics (Esmaeili Kelishomi et al., 2019):} Esmaeili Kelishomi et al.~\cite{kelishomi2019activities} showed that smartphone triaxial accelerometer features capture distinct indoor vs.\ outdoor locomotor kinematics (gait cadence, sustained velocity variance, and stationary posture distribution), complementing magnetic cues when a user walks outside near metallic vehicles or fences.
    \item \textbf{Causal Hidden Markov Model Smoothing (Zhu et al., 2019):} Zhu et al.~\cite{zhu2019fast} introduced a lightweight 2-state Hidden Markov Model (HMM) forward filter over frame-level IOD posteriors:
    \begin{equation}
        \alpha_t(s) \propto b_t(s) \sum_{s' \in \{\text{In},\text{Out}\}} \alpha_{t-1}(s')\, A_{s',s}
        \label{eq:hmm_lit}
    \end{equation}
    which suppresses single-second urban-canyon or pocket-swing flickers while achieving $3$--$4$\,s transition responsiveness without lookahead delay.
    \item \textbf{Path Straightness Index (Benhamou, 2004):} For GPS-assisted environment classification, Benhamou~\cite{benhamou2004tortuosity} formalized the path straightness index $S = d_{\text{net}} / d_{\text{path}} \in [0, 1]$, where $d_{\text{net}}$ is beeline displacement and $d_{\text{path}}$ is cumulative path length. Outdoor locomotion exhibits high straightness ($S \to 1$), whereas indoor multipath wander traces tortuous star-like loops ($S \to 0$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Inertial Step Detection, Stationary ZUPT, Cadence, and Stride Length}
\label{sec:cadence_literature}
When GNSS is unavailable or degraded (indoors, treadmills, tunnels), inertial pedometry provides primary distance and cadence telemetry.

\subsection{Causal Accelerometry Step Detection: Oxford WPD and Xu Running}
While native hardware step counters (\texttt{TYPE\_STEP\_COUNTER} on Android; \texttt{CMPedometer} on iOS) operate at ultra-low power, fallback and cross-platform validation require transparent causal software detectors:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Oxford Windowed Peak Detection (Salvi et al., 2018):} Salvi et al.~\cite{salvi2018oxford} resample triaxial acceleration magnitude $\|\mathbf{a}\|$ to $100$\,Hz, smooth with a Gaussian FIR filter, score local peaks over a sliding window, and gate detections using online Welford mean and standard deviation.
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Running Step Detection (Xu et al., 2022):} Xu et al.~\cite{xu2022step} target high-impact running gaits by combining $50$\,Hz Hamming low-pass filtering with 256-point DFT dominant-frequency extraction and variable sliding-window adaptive peak thresholding.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stationary Stance Rejection (MV-GLRT ZUPT) and Walk Detection}
A major failure mode of pure peak detectors on handheld smartphones is accumulating phantom steps during stationary screen taps or idle arm sway. Two classical papers provide statistically principled defenses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MV-GLRT Zero-Velocity Detection (Skog et al., 2010):} Evaluating four zero-velocity detectors on inertial gait streams, Skog et al.~\cite{skog2010zupt} derived the Magnitude-Variance Generalized Likelihood Ratio Test (MV-GLRT, Eq.~13):
    \begin{equation}
        T_{\text{MV}}(n) = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{k=n-W+1}^{n} \bigl(\|\mathbf{a}_k\|_2 - \bar{a}_n\bigr)^2 < \gamma_{\text{MV}}
        \label{eq:mv_glrt_lit}
    \end{equation}
    Because $T_{\text{MV}}(n)$ averages squared variance across the full window $W$ rather than relying on a single peak-to-peak excursion, isolated $50$\,ms screen taps or brief pocket bumps stay strictly below $\gamma_{\text{MV}}$ while sustained locomotion exceeds it cleanly.
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Smartphone Walk Detection (Brajdic \& Harle, 2013):} Brajdic and Harle~\cite{brajdic2013walk} benchmarked walk-detection and step-counting algorithms across unconstrained smartphone placements, showing that combining an acceleration standard-deviation floor with rhythmic step-periodicity confirmation ($[0.65, 4.5]$\,Hz) eliminates spurious non-walking bursts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Cadence Sliding-Window Trilemma and Adaptive Step Length}
Computing live cadence $C_W(t) = (\Delta M_{[t-W,t]} / W)\times 60$ from discrete steps embodies a trilemma across accuracy (evaluated against video-annotated PedEval~\cite{mattfeld2021pedeval} and OxWalk~\cite{oxwalk2022oxford}), visual display flutter, and stop decay latency. Once step frequency $f = C/60$ (Hz) is known, static stature rules ($L = kH$) systematically misestimate brisk walking and fast running. Renaudin et al.~\cite{renaudin2012step} (Eq.~8) formulate a linear height--frequency model:
\begin{equation}
    L_{\text{step}}(H, f) = H \cdot (a f + b) + c
    \label{eq:renaudin_lit}
\end{equation}
which can be calibrated via Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation on public walking (\texttt{SLEDataset2}~\cite{vezocnik2021sle}) and force-plate treadmill running (Fukuchi et al.~\cite{fukuchi2017public} RBDS) datasets.

\section{Sparse Trajectory Reconstruction and Map Compression}
\label{sec:reconstruction_literature}
Short GNSS outages ($2$--$15$\,s under overpasses or tree canopies) fracture visual route polylines. Czerwi{\'n}ski et al.~\cite{czerwinski2026reconstruction} present multi-stage offline wearable trajectory reconstruction, while Fritsch and Carlson~\cite{fritsch1980pchip} define shape-preserving Monotone Piecewise Cubic Hermite Interpolation (PCHIP) using boundary velocity derivatives, contrasted against simple Linear Chord interpolation. When outage boundary headings are corrupted by multipath, cubic splines can introduce lateral overshoot; Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} compares both methods against RTK truth. For compact client--server route transmission, variable-length delta-quantized Google Encoded Polylines reduce JSON bandwidth while bounding quantization error, and Hidden Markov Model road-network map matching~\cite{newson2009hidden} establishes formal server-side route correction contracts.

\section{Caloric Expenditure Models: 2024 Adult Compendium}
\label{sec:calorie_literature}
Standard exercise physiology estimates gross metabolic energy expenditure via Metabolic Equivalent of Task (MET) coefficients. Building upon the 2011 Compendium~\cite{ainsworth2011compendium}, Herrmann et al.~\cite{herrmann2024compendium} published the \textbf{2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities}, assigning exact 5-digit activity codes to discrete speed bands (in miles per hour) for walking, running, and cycling:
\begin{equation}
    E_{\text{kcal}} = \text{MET}(\text{activity}, v_{\text{mph}}) \times M_{\text{kg}} \times \frac{t_{\text{mov}}}{3600}
    \label{eq:compendium_2024}
\end{equation}
Using exact speed-band lookup tables gated strictly by active moving time $t_{\text{mov}}$ avoids unverified heuristic interpolation and enables bit-exact client--server reconciliation, while honestly acknowledging that Compendium values represent population-average estimates rather than individual $\dot{V}\text{O}_2$ indirect calorimetry.

\section{Research Gaps and Synthesis}
Synthesizing the literature reveals five concrete gaps addressed by Aetron:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lack of Independent RTK Holdout and Negative-Result Disclosure:} Complex filters (e.g., AEKF) are rarely audited across both pedestrian smartphone RTK and non-Doppler cycling snapshots under shared epoch masks with transparent \textit{NO-GO} decisions.
    \item \textbf{iOS Sensor Sandboxing Gap in Mobile IOD:} Published deep IOD architectures rely on cellular RSRP/RSRQ, raw $C/N_0$, or ambient light unavailable on iOS, leaving iPhone users with zero active IOD coverage unless light-free MagIO + inertial + CoreLocation + barometer + HMM fusion is engineered.
    \item \textbf{Uncalibrated Event-Level Doorway Transitions:} Static epoch accuracy obscures high hourly false-transition rates; both epoch Macro-F1 and event-level doorway F1 must be reported side by side.
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Stationary False Steps and Uncalibrated Cadence Windows:} Peak-only step counters leak false steps during idle phone handling, and live cadence windows are rarely audited against causal video-annotated stride oracles.
    \item \textbf{Ad-Hoc Heuristics Replacing Probabilistic Models:} Legacy linear scoring weights and static stride/calorie rules drift across speeds and devices; replacing them with DOI-verified statistical formulations (MV-GLRT, Benhamou straightness, Causal HMM, Renaudin LOSO, 2024 Compendium) bridges academic rigor and production reliability.
\end{enumerate}
